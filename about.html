About the community

The Ideal

A country run entirely by its members — written, argued, reported and voted since December 2024.

The founding

Built in December 2024

Peter and Mason started The American Ideal because the political roleplay they wanted did not exist yet: serious about procedure, generous to beginners, and hostile to nothing except bad behaviour.

The idea was simple. Write the institutions first — a legislature that actually keeps a calendar, an executive that can be checked, a bench that can say no — then let the members fill them. Nothing about the country is scripted. What happens next depends entirely on who shows up and what they argue for.

More than a year on, the simulation runs on its own rhythm: sessions, campaigns, appointments, scandals, and a press corps that writes it all down.

How the country is organised

The institutions

Three branches carry the simulation. Each has written rules, a public record, and members who hold their seats because someone put them there.

The Legislature

Members draft directives and bills, move them through committee, and take them to a recorded vote. Debate is in character and on the record — the transcript is what the press reports and what the wiki keeps.

The Executive

An elected administration that signs or vetoes, names its cabinet, and answers for the state of the country at the next election. The office is powerful; it is not permanent.

The Courts

Challenges are heard, argued and decided. A ruling can strike down a law that the other two branches were perfectly happy with — which is the point of having a bench at all.

The cycle

A session, start to finish

  • Step one

    Something gets proposed

    A member writes a directive or bill in the standard format and files it. Anyone can. You do not need a leadership role to start an argument.

  • Step two

    The floor takes it up

    Committee first where it applies, then debate, amendments, and a recorded vote. Whips count, deals get made, and some good bills die anyway.

  • Step three

    The press files

    Eagle Wire moves a story and officials get asked why they voted the way they did. The record outlives the session.

  • Step four

    The voters answer

    Campaign season comes around, records are read back to their owners, and the seats change hands. Then it starts again.
